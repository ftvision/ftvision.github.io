Now, Anthropic is required to stop providing access of Fable 5 to anyone who is not American.

Anthropic has repeatedly called for and executed access control, including asking U.S. government and society to work together to stop exporting GPUs to China, as well as stopping Claude acesss to OpenAI and previously XAI. In their recent essays, two themes have appeared

1. they are tired of neck-to-neck competitions from other labs to build LLMs
2. they think the only way they would be willing to conduct a conversation with other labs is to maintain a significant competitive advantage over the LLMs built by other groups

they seems to believe Access Control would brings a competitive moat, and the competitive moat gives them not only the leadership, but also the negotiation stake on the table. 

It is true that Claude has been doing very well on the coding and coding agent, and it is fair to say they are a leading edge. It is also fair to say they have defined multiple industrial standard and protocols, including Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Skills. 

However, I want to argue: Access Denial is not your moat, and you should start conversations now instead of waiting for a sweet period when you are comfortably ahead and everyone else. Because you will never have that comfortable period.


## holding the access to coding model

It was clear, in retrospect, that Anthropic has betted on the coding model quite early. Ever since sonnot 3.6, it was my favorite coding model and that didn't change until Opus 4.6 vs GPT 5.5. There are two interesting facts: 
a) OpenAI was the first company who demonstrated the coding capability via Codex model, way way back at 2022 (TODO:check). Somehow they didn't pursue this line and it was not until GPT 5.3 when they broung codex back (TODO: check)
b) OpenAI's employees were using Claude and Claude Code happily and doesn't seem to be motivated to focus on their coding-specific model training until ANthropic cut their access.

In a sense, from the perspective of outsider. It is the exact action of access control that prioritized the coding model capability improvement in OpenAI and gave anthropic neck-to-neck competition. While they stopped access to Openai, they also stopped their access to Xai, which forced Xai to also prioritze training their coding model. Of course, XAI didn't deliver, and they brought Cursor in, now Cursor does have a very good Composer 2.5

## holding the access to GPU

Anthropic repeatedly calls for stop exporting GPUs to China, with quite a long essay on the dystopic future of having Chinese companies who are able to deliver frontier-like model to the world. The are two interesting facts as well
a) Turns out the world is benefiting from Chinese open-weight model a lot and many American companies and people are using Chinese open weight model. It is not clear what the alternative universe would look like, but we don't see any other American companies publishing open weight models that is able to catch up with them close-weight frontier model, nor do we see any other country has published open weight models, yet, to benefit the rest of the world. 
b) It is exactly the repeated call for access control that prioritize and force the entire Chinese industrial supplychain to accelerate their work on providing the entire stack for model training, including chips, memories, and algorithms. It is possible that this force from U.S. has put the schedule five years earlier than it would have planned. Jensen Huang, in his interviews, has clearly understood this: Providing the GPU access to China may actually delay them from building their own chips while maintaining U.S. leadership and monopoly on the tech-stack ecosystem. Regardless, this view of trying to access deny as a moat has some serious problem in the underlying assumptionts.

### 1) The AGI is a short-term game with clear end line.

It appears that there is a clear "AGI" endpoint, where one company rush to the end, and would be able to maintain a constant, clear competitive edge against other companies. If you ever wonder, this is definitely wrong. 

OpenAI was way ahead during the chatgpt time, and that gave Google a very hard time, not to mention other companies like Amazon, Meta. However, if you look at the horizon of 3 years, Google has caught up successfully, and even imposed threats on OpenAI. Similarly, anthropic seemed to be leading coding for more than a year, but if you look at a longer horizon, you see GPT 5.5 has caught up and Codex has got a lot of users praise than Claude Code recently. Gemini, though is lagging behind, should not be treated as failed already. 

Somehow I feel people are quite short-sighted and want to declare victory quickly. It happens a lot in the Sillcon Valley nowadays. The narratives seem to be we need to have a headsstart and leading. Startups want to lead 3-month performence over big labs, claiming their competitive advantage. But heads-start doesn't necessarily consolidate into your moat, you just happen to start a bit earlier. if you have ever watched an ordinary person try to swim competition agains a professional swimmer, you may have seen that the professional swimmer would just let the ordinary person start earlier for even more than 10 seconds, then you see they just very quickly close the gap and then win. 

The human society has last for tens of thousand years and it will continue to last for tens of thousands years. one or two years leading may give you a good feeling and temporary advantage, but you couldn't and shouldn't pretend that there is a crossing-bar and you can rest. for hte observers, you should also not expect any single company will hold an absolute edge constantly. That will be a very sad world.


### 2) The information 

People not living in a vacuum. No society lives in a vaccum either. It is an arrogant view to think people in one group are the only people who knows the secret of building soem single most important invention for the humanity, and holding the people and the resource will prevent other people to independently figure out the secret. 

It was never the case. All inventions in the human history have been independently discovered more than once, even in the ancient time when information flows much slower, in narrower range. Nowadays, information flows at the speed of light. People also moves around. It is the nature and basic principles of how market works. 

With information flowing, the knowledge of how to build large models will be broadcast in a large range. Ambitious people exist around the world, even though America does attract a good amount of it.

### 3) Start the conversation early

It appears that Anthropic has built on a strong distrust of OpenAI, but at the same time, the way they want to start the conversation doesn't help the conversation get started. Why not starting the conversation now? if you continue to distrust OpenAI, when would you ever want to sit down on the same table and engage your peels? 

The human history has seen enough tradegy that delay the conversation, also have seen enough great things when the conversations are conducted, even tho the process is difficult and has lots of back and force. No negotion is easy, no matter how advanced you are, no matter how stronger you are against the counterpart. No one would be happily enter a conversation where the other side declares an unfair advantage -- that is not an fair negotiation in the first place. Certainly, chinese people won't be happy to be that position.

- Topic sentence: When you deny a competitor access, you do not freeze them — you change their priorities, converting a convenient dependency into an urgent vulnerability.

### Arc 3 — The Isolation Fallacy
